Section 2 — Themes 

Theme 1: Delivery Reliability Is Eroding Customer Trust
Who mentioned it: Rohan, Anjali, Vikram
Anchor quote:
“75 minutes! And this isn't a one-off. I've timed it. Between 7-9 PM in Bangalore, if I order Zomato, I'm gambling.” — Rohan
Why it matters:
 Delivery delays are not just operational problems—they are affecting trust, repeat usage, and customer perception. Rohan has started checking Swiggy first, Anjali abandoned Zomato after her first bad delivery, and Vikram describes delivery times as steadily worsening.
Theme 2: Peak-Time Demand Is Exceeding Available Supply Capacity
Who mentioned it: Rohan, Meera, Vikram
Anchor quote:
“My kitchen isn't scaled for both walk-in surge and delivery. Nobody's.” — Meera
Why it matters:
 The interviews suggest that peak-time problems are not simply caused by "too many orders." There is a capacity mismatch across the ecosystem. Rohan and Vikram experience the customer-side impact during Bangalore's 7–9 PM rush, while Meera explains the restaurant-side constraint: weekend walk-ins overwhelm kitchen capacity, resulting in cancellations.

Theme 3: Customers Are Highly Sensitive to Unexpected Costs
Who mentioned it: Priya, Rohan, Karthik
Anchor quote:
“Only at checkout. Which is annoying. I've already invested time picking food. Then boom — fee revealed. It feels like a trap. Sunk cost is real.” — Priya
Why it matters:
 Users are not only reacting to the absolute price—they are reacting to unexpected or changing value. Priya abandons orders when delivery fees cross her ₹40 threshold, while Rohan and Karthik describe Gold's restrictions and additional fees as reducing the value they expected.
Note: This theme is slightly broader than "delivery fees"; the common pattern is price/value transparency and predictability.

Theme 4: Changes to the Value Proposition Are Creating a Trust Break
Who mentioned it: Rohan, Karthik, Vikram
Anchor quote:
“But hiding changes behind fine print? That's a trust break. I'll never be a full-price advocate for them again.” — Karthik
Why it matters:
 Subscription changes are causing more than cancellations. Users feel that the value they originally signed up for has been quietly reduced. Rohan's monthly savings fell from around ₹500 to ₹150, Karthik tracked his savings declining from ₹480 to ₹180, and Vikram also cancelled because the value dropped without warning.

Theme 5: First Experiences Have a Disproportionate Impact on Long-Term Retention
Who mentioned it: Anjali, Vikram, Rohan
Anchor quote:
“First orders should be sacred — like a job interview. You get one shot. And they blew it.” — Anjali
Why it matters:
 A poor first experience can prevent a customer from ever developing a relationship with the platform. Anjali uninstalled Zomato after her first order, while Vikram describes his cousin doing the same after a terrible first delivery. Rohan's experience shows the longer-term version of this problem: repeated poor experiences eventually turned Zomato into his backup app.

Section 4 — Problem Statements 

1. Bangalore Evening Delivery Reliability
When ordering dinner between 7–9 PM in Bangalore, urban working professionals want their food delivered within the promised timeframe, but delivery times become significantly longer and unreliable during the evening peak, creating a gap between the promised and actual experience — evidenced by QuickBites data showing 7–9 PM Bangalore orders averaging 61 minutes versus 31 minutes elsewhere, and 2/6 interviews (Rohan and Vikram) explicitly describing 60+ minute evening deliveries.

2. Weekend Restaurant Fulfillment Capacity
When customers order from South Indian restaurants on weekends, customers want their orders to be fulfilled reliably, but restaurants struggle to handle the combined demand from walk-ins and delivery orders, resulting in significantly higher cancellations — evidenced by QuickBites data showing a 22.7% weekend cancellation rate for South Indian restaurants versus 10.3% on weekdays, and interviews with Meera and Vikram confirming weekend fulfillment problems.

3. Unexpected Delivery Fees
When price-sensitive customers select a relatively low-priced meal, they want to understand the true cost before committing to the order, but delivery charges revealed late in the journey can make the final price feel unexpectedly high and cause customers to abandon checkout — evidenced by QuickBites data showing cart completion falling to 32% at ₹40+ delivery fees, and Priya explicitly saying, “Above 40, I usually bail” and that the fee “feels like a trap.”
Note: This one has WEAK interview triangulation under the strict 3-user rule because Priya is the only interviewee who directly discusses the ₹40 threshold. However, her response closely matches the quantitative threshold.

4. Gold Subscription Value Erosion
When using a Gold subscription, subscribers want the benefits and savings they signed up for to remain valuable, but changes to eligibility, minimum order values and additional charges have reduced the perceived value of the subscription, leading to cancellations — evidenced by QuickBites data showing Gold retention falling from 73% for the pre-June cohort to 38% for the post-June cohort, and 3/6 interviews (Rohan, Karthik and Vikram) describing cancellation and declining Gold value.

5. Poor First-Order Experience and Repeat Usage
When a new customer places their first food-delivery order, they want a reliable experience that builds trust in the platform, but delivery failures, late arrivals, poor food condition or missing items can prevent them from placing a second order — evidenced by QuickBites data identifying users with total_orders_lifetime = 1 who never returned, and 2/6 interviews (Anjali and Vikram) directly connecting a poor first delivery experience with uninstalling or not returning
